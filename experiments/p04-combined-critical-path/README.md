# p04 - Combined critical path: ORIGO proof + open bonded issuers + objective misuse revocation

- **Purpose:** running evidence, on a local EVM chain, that the ORIGO zero-knowledge attestation (e05) and
  the sound core of Paprika (open admission with a stake; revocation by anyone with objective evidence,
  paid from the stake) work together in **one contract with no owner, admin, committee or upgrade path**.
- **Result:** all 9 tests pass with 10 real UltraHonk proofs generated at test time
  (raw output: [`output/2026-11-20-run.txt`](output/2026-11-20-run.txt)). Two design problems of the
  *combination* are listed first below; they are not bugs in the code, they are limits of the idea.
- **Environment:** Linux x86_64, Node 26.4.0, `nargo` 1.0.0, `bb` 6.0.0, Hardhat 2.29.2 (in-process chain,
  hardfork `osaka`, chainId 31337, default test accounts), solc 0.8.28 (optimizer on, 1 run, evm target
  cancun), ethers 6. 2026-11-20.

## Design flaws found (read first)

1. **Open admission + unlinkable proofs = anyone can self-issue "devices" for 1 ETH.** Anyone can
   `registerIssuer()`, publish a root of keys it holds, and produce attestations that are cryptographically
   identical to a real manufacturer's. The bond is not at risk for this: there is no objective evidence of
   "fake manufacturer" (phantom leaves are undetectable, review O-09), so nothing can slash it. The bond
   only prices *Sybil issuers*, it does not make any issuer trustworthy. Consequence: an attestation from
   this contract means only "a key under a root published by bonded address X signed this". A verifier
   **must** apply its own list of trusted issuers (curators, review O-03 option 1) to `statusOf(tag).issuer`.
   Without that layer the registry certifies nothing about photo origin.
2. **A "misuse proof" proves that the key signs outside the capture format, which on a keystore-level
   device is always possible for the device owner.** The test requirement ("a key that can sign captures
   can also be made to sign this") is exactly the problem: the simulated device has one signing interface
   (ECDSA-SHA256 over a payload), so its *owner* can sign the misuse payload, revoke their own device and
   collect `BOUNTY` from the issuer's bond. With 16 devices x 0.25 ETH against a 1 ETH bond, four owners
   drain an honest issuer. The mechanism is objective and front-run-proof, but it means "key extracted or
   misused" only if the device firmware/secure element refuses to sign anything that does not start with
   `"ORIGO-P/CAPTURE/v1"` and does not contain a sensor-computed digest (sensor-level signing, review
   O-06/O-10). Otherwise the bounty must be smaller than what an owner loses by revoking their own
   device, and the issuer's bond cap must be sized for that. Conversely, a forger who extracted a key
   simply never publishes a misuse signature: revocation only fires when the key *leaks further*.

Further limits are in "What this combination still does not solve".

## What is signed (byte layout)

The device has **one** signing interface: standard ECDSA P-256 with SHA-256 over a byte payload
(`crypto.sign("sha256", payload)`, IEEE P1363 `r || s`, normalised to low-s because the circuit requires it).
As in e05, the circuit checks the signature against `m = SHA-256(payload)`, so the contract only has to
recompute `m`.

| Payload | Bytes | Layout (all `abi.encodePacked`, fixed width) |
|---|---|---|
| capture | 134 | `"ORIGO-P/CAPTURE/v1"` (18 ASCII) ‖ `chainid` (32, big-endian uint256) ‖ `address(this)` (20) ‖ `imageDigest` (32) ‖ `blockhash(anchorBlock)` (32) |
| misuse | 89 | `"ORIGO-P/MISUSE/v1"` (17 ASCII) ‖ `chainid` (32) ‖ `address(this)` (20) ‖ `challenger = msg.sender` (20) |

- `m = SHA-256(capture payload)` is computed by the contract in `attest` (`captureMessage`) and passed to
  the verifier as public input (`m_hi, m_lo, root_hi, root_lo, tag_hi, tag_lo`, 128-bit halves).
- The misuse hash `SHA-256(misuse payload)` is computed by the contract and checked with the P-256
  precompile at `0x100` (`hash ‖ r ‖ s ‖ x ‖ y`).
- Cross-domain use is impossible because both hashes are computed by the contract from payloads that
  differ from byte 9 on (`C` vs `M`) and have different fixed lengths; passing one as the other would need
  a SHA-256 collision. Tested in one direction (genuine capture signature submitted as a misuse proof:
  rejected). The other direction needs no test: the circuit only accepts a signature over the `m` that the
  contract computed with the capture label.
- The anchor **block number** is not in the payload; the 32-byte block hash identifies the block. The
  record stores the caller-supplied `anchorBlock`, which is safe because `blockhash(anchorBlock)` must
  reproduce the signed hash.

## Contract behaviour (summary)

- **Issuers:** `registerIssuer()` (payable, `>= MIN_BOND = 1 ETH`, once per address). `publishRoot(root)`:
  active issuer, bond `>= MIN_BOND`, root non-zero and globally unique, stores issuer and block.
  `deactivateRoot(root)`: only its issuer, permanent, stores the block. `requestExit()` then
  `withdrawBond()` after `UNBONDING_BLOCKS = 100`.
- **attest:** `anchorBlock < block.number`; `blockhash(anchorBlock) != 0` (covers future, current and
  > 256-block-old anchors); root published and active now; tag not yet recorded; tag not that of a revoked
  key of this root (see below); `verifier.verify(proof, inputs)`; then stores
  `{imageDigest, root, m, anchorBlock (not-before), block.number (not-after)}` under `tag`, never deleted,
  and appends the tag to `tagsOf(imageDigest)`. The relayer address is not stored.
- **proveKeyMisuse:** recomputes leaf and depth-10 path with the circuit's hashes, requires the result to be
  a published root (active or not), checks the P-256 signature over the misuse hash naming `msg.sender`,
  records the key (pk, block) and pays `min(BOUNTY = 0.25 ETH, remaining bond)` of the root's issuer.
- **statusOf(tag):** the record, the issuer, root publication block, `rootInactiveSince` (0 = active), and
  whether the record's tag belongs to a key revoked under that root and since which block - computed
  **on-chain** by looping over the root's revoked keys and recomputing `SHA-256(0x02 ‖ pk ‖ m)`. The record
  stores `m` because `blockhash` is gone after 256 blocks.

## Design notes and deviations

1. **New attestation by a revoked key: the contract rejects it on its own.** The key is hidden in the
   proof, but the tag is a public function of `(pk, m)` and the contract knows `m` and the revoked keys of
   the claimed root, so `attest` recomputes the tag for each revoked key of that root and reverts with
   `key revoked`. Cost: about 5.5 k gas per revoked key under the root (measured 4,209,589 vs 4,204,077
   gas with 1 vs 0). Bounded by the tree size: at most 1,024 keys per root, about +5.6 M gas worst case,
   still under the 16.7 M per-transaction gas cap of EIP-7825 (osaka) [from memory; Hardhat enforcement not checked]. The same tag check is also possible off-chain
   (shown in the test) for verifiers that do not trust the contract's view.
2. **Revocation is per key, time-scoped, never erases records** (O-02): `keyRevokedAt` is the block of the
   first misuse proof; records before it keep their status and are reported as "attested at b, key proven
   misusable at N > b". No retroactive "compromised since" field (no one is entitled to set it).
3. **A key that is a leaf of several roots** is revoked once globally (`keyRevokedAt[leaf]`, one bounty),
   but must be listed under each root for the per-root loop. A further misuse proof for the same key under
   another root lists it there without paying. Until someone does that, the other root still accepts it.
   Issuers should never reuse a key across roots; the contract cannot check this.
4. **Exit is prospective for all of the issuer's roots:** from the `requestExit` block, `rootInactiveSince`
   returns that block for every root of the issuer, so no new attestations; misuse proofs keep paying
   until `withdrawBond`. After withdrawal they still revoke but pay 0 (tested).
5. **Bond drained by bounties:** roots stay active even at bond 0 (only `publishRoot` requires
   `bond >= MIN_BOND`). No top-up function. An alternative is "roots inactive below MIN_BOND", which would
   let bounty claims (including self-revocations, flaw 2) shut an issuer down. Left open deliberately.
6. **Misuse payload does not name the root or the key** (the signature is by the key, so it is bound to it).
   ECDSA malleability (`s` vs `n - s`) is harmless: a key is listed once per root.
7. **Tag uniqueness only, several tags per image** (O-07). `tagsOf(imageDigest)` returns all of them; the
   earliest not-after is the meaningful "existed before".
8. **The verifier reverts** with custom errors (for example `SumcheckFailed()`) instead of returning false;
   `attest` propagates the revert. The tests decode the error name.
9. **Test harness note:** ethers 6 caches identical reads for 250 ms; the first run reported a wrong
   bounty balance because two `getBalance` calls hit the cache. The tests disable the cache
   (`cacheTimeout: -1`). The contract was not changed.

## What this combination still does not solve

- **Who is a manufacturer.** See flaw 1: open admission removes the gatekeeper and also removes any
  meaning of "registered issuer". Trust lists move to the verifier. The contract exposes `issuer` so
  that this is possible; it does not provide the lists.
- **Forgery by an issuer or by whoever holds an extracted key and keeps it private** is not detectable,
  so neither revocation nor the bond touches it (O-09, O-13).
- **Self-revocation economics** (flaw 2). Unsolved without restricted signing in hardware.
- **Deanonymisation on revocation.** The misuse proof publishes `pk`; `statusOf` then links every record of
  that key, past and future. Time-scoping fixes censorship (no retroactive invalidation), not linking.
- **Not-before is vacuous on a local single-node chain** (the operator knows block hashes first) and the
  256-block window excludes offline devices (no checkpoint / EIP-2935 storage here, O-06).
- **Cost and throughput:** about 4.2 M gas per photo (O-04); no batching.
- **The anonymity set is one batch**; the root reveals issuer and batch (O-05).
- **Key/firmware vs capture provenance** (O-10) and **unmeasured phone proving** (O-12) are untouched.
- **The submitter of `attest` is visible on-chain**; without a relayer the device owner's address links
  their photos.
- Generated verifier and this contract are unaudited; device keys are simulated.
