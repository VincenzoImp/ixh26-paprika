# Hard questions, with short honest answers

For the 2 minutes of questions. Source of truth: `docs/1-come-funziona.md`; context in `docs/2` and
`docs/3`. Not claim-audited.

**1. What stops a software key attesting an AI image?**
Nothing at attestation time. Any registered key, hardware or software, can sign any bytes, and the
contract cannot tell what they are. The only consequence is economic: the attester loses `B` if
someone proves the image fake with public evidence within `T_photo`. That is why we do not claim
physical origin.

**2. What evidence can voters actually check?**
Only evidence that anyone can verify publicly. Examples: the same image published before the
record's block window; the image regenerated byte-for-byte from a known model and seed; content
that contradicts public facts. UMA's rules give "no" when a claim cannot be resolved, so a
well-made fake that leaves no such trace is not caught.

**3. Why is the ban free to evade?**
Registration is free, so a flagged party registers a new key. What the flag costs is the deposit
on the proven fake and the device's record of attestations, which verifiers can read. A registration deposit would
price evasion, but it would also exclude people; the team chose a per-photo deposit instead, so
spam and fakes are priced per photo.

**4. Why UMA, and what if the vote is bought?**
UMA has a deployed contract interface (Optimistic Oracle V3) that a contract can call, and its
rules require public verifiability. The vote's security is UMA's, not ours: whoever controls
enough votes can steer a decision. The damage is bounded: one verdict decides one accusation, moves
at most `B`, flags one device, and erases nothing. A different arbiter, such as Kleros, would have to
be chosen before deployment, because the contract cannot be modified afterwards.

**5. Why commit-reveal?**
Pending transactions are public. A one-step accusation could be copied by a front-runner, who would
take the reward, or pre-empted by the forger's own second account to cap its loss. The commitment
hides the photo and binds the accuser's address, so a copy cannot be revealed by anyone else.
Someone who commits and does not reveal within `T_reveal` loses the bond.

**6. What does the block hash prove?**
It gives a lower bound on the time of signing: the hash could not be known before that block
existed, so the signature was made after it. The block that holds the record gives the upper bound.
It bounds the signing time, not the capture time: at the protocol level nothing stops someone
signing an old file today. A block producer can bias a hash slightly, but cannot know it far in
advance.

**7. Why equal bonds?**
UMA requires the asserter's bond and the disputer's bond to be equal. The contract asserts with the
accuser's bond and disputes with the photo's deposit, so both are `B`. The symmetry also makes the
two possible mistakes cost the same: a false accusation loses `B`, and a proven fake loses `B`.

**8. What would you add first with more time?**
A real-network deployment, with `B` and `T_photo` sized to the value of what is being protected.
Unlinkable records, for example a zero-knowledge proof that the signer is some registered,
unflagged device. And a way to make self-accusation more expensive than `fB`. We would also
revisit the arbiter, for example a jury drawn from Paprika participants, to reduce dependence on
an external vote.
